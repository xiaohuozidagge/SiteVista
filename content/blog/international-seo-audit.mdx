Expanding a website to serve multiple countries or languages introduces a layer of complexity that domestic SEO does not have to contend with. International SEO is not just about translation — it is about ensuring that the right version of each page appears to the right users in the right markets, and that search engines understand the relationship between your regional and language variants.

This guide covers the full international audit, from hreflang through reporting. If you would rather have this done for you, see our [international SEO audit service](/international-seo-audit-services/) or the broader [website SEO audit service](/seo-audit/).

## Hreflang Reciprocity

Hreflang tags tell search engines which language and regional audience each page is intended for, and they are the most commonly broken element in international SEO.

Reciprocity means that if the English page points to the French page, the French page must point back to the English page. Broken reciprocity is the single most frequent cause of hreflang-related ranking confusion, because search engines tend to ignore a cluster that does not resolve in both directions.

**Good:** EN page references FR and DE; FR references EN and DE; DE references EN and FR.

**Bad:** EN references FR and DE, but FR only references DE. The cluster is incomplete and may be ignored.

## Self-Referencing Hreflang

Every page should include an hreflang annotation for its own language and region combination, alongside the annotations for its alternates.

A missing self-reference is a common oversight that can break the entire cluster. Google's documentation explicitly requires each page to include itself in its hreflang set.

**Good:** the English page lists `hreflang="en"` for itself, plus `fr`, `de`, and `es` for its alternates.

**Bad:** the English page lists only `fr`, `de`, and `es` — omitting itself.

## Valid Language and Country Codes

Hreflang codes must use a valid ISO 639-1 language code, optionally followed by an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country code. The language code is required; the country code is optional and narrows the targeting.

Check for invented or malformed codes such as `hreflang="eng"` (should be `en`), `hreflang="uk"` (should be `en-gb`), or `hreflang="en-uk"` (should be `en-gb`). A typo in a code silently disables the annotation.

## Canonical and Hreflang Conflicts

A page that is canonicalized to a different language version while also participating in a hreflang cluster sends contradictory signals. The canonical and hreflang must agree.

**Good:** the French page is self-canonical (`rel=canonical` → the French URL) and points its hreflang alternates to each language version.

**Bad:** the French page's canonical points to the English version while its hreflang cluster lists the French URL. Search engines are left to guess which signal wins.

Audit that every URL in a hreflang cluster is itself the canonical for its own version.

## Redirected or Noindex Hreflang URLs

Hreflang annotations must point to the final, canonical URL. An hreflang that points to a redirected URL, a noindexed page, or a page blocked by robots.txt is ignored.

**Bad:** the French hreflang points to an old URL that 301-redirects to the new French page. The annotation should reference the destination, not the redirect.

Crawl every URL in your hreflang map and confirm each returns 200, is indexable, and is self-canonical.

## Multilingual XML Sitemaps

Each language version should be discoverable through your XML sitemaps, either in a single sitemap or in per-language sitemap files.

Check that the sitemap includes only indexable, canonical URLs and does not reference redirected or noindexed versions. For larger sites, per-language sitemaps with hreflang annotations inside the URL entries make the relationships explicit.

## Language and Regional URL Structure

The three common structures each have tradeoffs. Country-code top-level domains (`.de`, `.fr`) provide the strongest geo-signal but are the most expensive to operate. Subdomains (`fr.example.com`) are easier to set up but do not inherit the full authority of the main domain. Subdirectories (`example.com/fr/`) consolidate authority and are generally the easiest to maintain.

Whatever structure you choose, apply it consistently. Mixing subdomains for some languages and subdirectories for others confuses both users and search engines.

## International Keyword Research

A keyword that performs well in English may have a completely different search volume and intent in another language. Direct translations often miss the terms people actually use.

Research keywords independently in each language, not by translating your English keyword list. Check that the translated term actually has search volume and matches the same intent. This is the foundation for the localized content work that follows.

## Localized Title, Description, and H1

Key on-page elements must be translated and optimized per market, not just carried over from the primary language.

**Good:** a German page whose title, meta description, and H1 were researched in German and reflect German search terms.

**Bad:** a German page whose title is a literal translation of the English title, ignoring how German searchers phrase the query.

Audit that every language version has unique, localized titles, descriptions, and headings — not machine-copied duplicates.

## Machine Translation Quality

Machine-translated content without human review reads poorly and produces high bounce rates, which signal low quality to search engines.

**Good:** translation produced by a machine but reviewed and edited by a native speaker who adapts examples, units, and tone.

**Bad:** raw machine translation with mistranslated idioms and untranslated currency or measurement units.

Localization goes beyond translation: adapt cultural references, currency, date formats, and examples so they are relevant to the target audience.

## Navigation Between Language Versions

Language and regional variants should be linked through a navigation element users can find and use — a language selector in the header or footer, ideally using `<link rel="alternate" hreflang>` rather than only JavaScript.

Avoid forcing redirects based on IP address or browser language without giving users the option to override and stay on their chosen version. Forced redirects can prevent search engines from crawling alternate language versions.

## International Internal Linking

Internal links should connect related content across language versions where it genuinely helps the user, not link every page to every translation of every other page.

**Good:** a French product page linking to the French category page, and the French version of a related guide.

**Bad:** a French page with no internal links to other French content, forcing users back to the English site for everything.

Anchor text should be in the language of the target page and describe it naturally.

## Geo-Specific Trust Signals

Search engines use multiple signals to determine geographic relevance. Country-code domains are the strongest; for generic domains, the International Targeting setting in Google Search Console lets you set a country preference.

Local signals reinforce targeting: local business schema, consistent name-address-phone data on location pages, local currency and contact details, and links from sites in the target country. Audit that these signals are present and consistent for each market.

## Indexation by Country and Language

Use Search Console's international reports to verify that each language version is actually indexed and receiving impressions in its target market.

**Bad:** a German version that is crawled but never indexed, meaning German searchers never see it regardless of how good the content is.

Check index coverage per language directory or subdomain, and investigate any language version that is excluded or receiving no impressions.

## International SEO Reporting

International SEO reporting should be segmented by market. A single country's aggregate traffic hides whether one market is thriving while another is failing.

Report on impressions, clicks, and rankings per country and language. Track hreflang errors over time, and monitor that each market's pages are indexed and gaining visibility. This is how you catch a market that silently breaks before it costs you revenue.

## International SEO Checklist

Run these checks for each language and region version of your site.

| Check | What to Review | Common Problem | Recommended Fix | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hreflang reciprocity | Every referenced page points back | One-way annotations | Make annotations reciprocal | High |
| Self-referencing hreflang | Each page lists itself | Missing self-reference | Add self-reference to every page | High |
| Language/region codes | Valid ISO codes | Invalid codes like `eng` or `uk` | Use correct ISO codes | High |
| Canonical vs hreflang | Canonical and hreflang agree | Canonical points to another language | Make each page self-canonical | High |
| Hreflang target status | Targets return 200 and are indexable | Redirected or noindexed targets | Point to final canonical URLs | High |
| XML sitemaps | Per-language URLs included | Missing language versions | Add all indexable URLs | Medium |
| URL structure | One consistent structure | Mixed subdomains and subdirectories | Standardize on one structure | Medium |
| Keyword research | Researched per language | Direct translation of keywords | Research each market separately | High |
| Localized metadata | Unique titles/descriptions/H1 | Literal translations | Localize and optimize per market | High |
| Translation quality | Human-reviewed, localized | Raw machine translation | Review and adapt per market | High |
| Language navigation | Visible, crawlable selector | JS-only or forced redirects | Use hreflang link elements | Medium |
| Internal linking | Links within each language | No links between same-language pages | Link related same-language pages | Medium |
| Geo trust signals | Local schema, NAP, links | No local signals | Add consistent local signals | Medium |
| Indexation by market | Each version indexed and visible | German version not indexed | Investigate coverage per market | High |
| Reporting | Segmented by country/language | Aggregate-only reporting | Report per market | Low |
